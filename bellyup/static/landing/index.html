The food is thrown away. The need is measured. The matching layer was missing.

BellyUp connects small restaurants with surplus food to the agencies that collect it and the mobile pantries that deliver it — routed by a real spatial-statistics model of where unsheltered need is concentrated tonight, and where it's headed next.

The problem

The bottleneck isn't supply. It's a matching layer that doesn't exist yet.

The USDA estimates 30–40% of the US food supply goes to waste, while people two blocks from full kitchens go hungry. Meanwhile downtown San Diego's own street count fell 24.8% in the year after a 2023 camping ordinance — a number that reads as both fewer people sleeping outside and people whose location simply changed. BellyUp doesn't editorialize that; it routes food to wherever the count says people actually are.

The prediction layer

Getis-Ord Gi* tells you where need clusters. A trend model tells you where it's headed.

Most of the map is real, present-tense need — not a forecast. Gi* is a spatial statistic: it tests whether a block's need, combined with its neighbors' within 250m, is a genuine cluster or just noise (z > 1.96, p < 0.05). Only statistically significant clusters ever reach the map — everything else is filtered out as noise, not just dimmed.

Gi* = (Σwijxj − X̄Σwij) / (S·√[(nΣwij² − (Σwij)²)/(n−1)])
trend = OLS slope over each block's last 5 counts (numpy.polyfit)
reward = meals × $4.25 × accessBoost × forecastBoost × freshness − cost(fuel, time)

Real clusters, not raw counts

A block with 23 people but no statistical cluster (z=1.73) is still fed — Gi* touches preference in the reward, never eligibility in who qualifies. The two questions stay separate on purpose.

Emerging vs. cooling, forecast forward

Each cluster's trend classifies it emerging (top quartile growth), cooling (bottom quartile), or established — and a p-value-gated area regression cross-checks the finding before it ever reaches the reward.

Routed through real streets

Delivery routes draw through actual road geometry via OSRM, not straight lines — with an automatic straight-line fallback if the routing service is ever slow or unreachable, so a flaky public API never breaks the map.

One glance, not a spreadsheet

An operations dashboard aggregates tonight's report pipeline, pending requests, and the prediction model's current read — computed once, server-side, so it can never disagree with what the map shows.

Ethics, unprompted

The map that can't be used to find people.

Hotspots are agency-facing only. The restaurant and public views never see outreach locations — only agencies, whose job is distribution, can see where need is.

Block-level aggregation, not addresses. Need is measured and shown per city block, never as an individual's location — the output can't be operationalized to find a specific person.

The pantry finder never claims a site is empty. It says a pantry has no BellyUp deliveries logged today — not that it has no food — because the ledger only knows what this platform routed.
