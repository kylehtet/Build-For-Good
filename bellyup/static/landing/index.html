How it works

A restaurant reports surplus

Log tonight's leftover food in about a minute — no account, no liability, and an enhanced tax deduction in return. Then dinner service goes back to being dinner service.

An agency picks it up

The best-matched collector gets the run: a multi-stop route through real streets, planned on cost, freshness, and where tonight's need is — and where it's growing.

Someone finds food nearby

The public map shows open pantries within walking distance — and only pantries. It never shows the outreach locations where people actually are.

The map underneath

Routed by statistics, not hunches

Every evening BellyUp maps where need clusters, block by block — tested against noise, so only real clusters count — and where each one is headed next. Then food follows the count: real streets, real numbers, and a plan that updates as the city does.

Our promises

The map can't find people

Where need concentrates is visible only to the agencies who deliver. Restaurants and the public never see outreach locations — that view doesn't exist for them.

Blocks, never addresses

Need is measured and shown per city block, never as anyone's location. The map can point food toward a neighborhood; it can never point at a person.

We never call a pantry empty

The finder only knows what BellyUp routed. It will say a pantry has no deliveries logged today — never that there's no food. Go look; kitchens surprise you.
